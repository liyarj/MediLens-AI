About the Project

One interface, three ways to look closer.

MediLens AI brings chest X-ray, CT, and ECG screening into a single workflow — built to explore whether a shared, explainable interface can make early multimodal screening more accessible, not to replace clinical diagnosis.

The Concept

Most screening tools are built around a single modality — one model, one scan type, one narrow use case. In practice, a clinician (or a patient trying to make sense of their own results) often has more than one type of scan to think about at once. MediLens AI's core idea is simple: bring chest X-ray, CT, and ECG analysis into one interface, so a person can upload what they have, see a per-scan result with a plain-language explanation of what drove it, and then see a combined picture across everything they've uploaded.

Each modality runs its own dedicated model — a chest X-ray classifier, a CT-based COVID/non-COVID classifier, and an ECG arrhythmia classifier trained on signal-derived features. Results are shown with their confidence score and, where available, the specific signals the model weighted most heavily, rather than a single opaque "risk number."

MediLens AI is explicitly a screening-support tool, not a diagnostic device. Confidence scores reflect how strongly a model's output matches patterns it was trained on for that modality — they are not a calibrated probability of disease, and none of the current models adjust for patient-specific factors like age, height, or weight. That's a known limitation, and one of the open directions for the project.

Chest X-Ray
Image-based classification, flagging visual patterns worth a closer look.
ECG Signal
Rhythm classification from signal-derived features like HRV, RMSSD, and R-R intervals.
CT Scan
Transfer-learned image classifier trained to distinguish COVID from non-COVID scans.
Why this matters: individually, X-ray, CT, and ECG classifiers already exist as research projects. What's less explored is combining them into one explainable, multimodal interface aimed at early screening rather than final diagnosis — the gap this project sits in.
Research This Builds On
Most directly related Closest analog
A CNN-FCN-LSTM Multimodal Framework for Automatic Disease Diagnosis
Diagnostics (MDPI), 2023
The closest existing analog to MediLens — combines CNN (image), FCN, and LSTM (signal) branches into one multimodal diagnostic pipeline, mirroring our per-modality-model + combined-output approach.
Multimodal fusion
Multimodal fusion of imaging and genomics for cancer diagnosis
npj Digital Medicine, 2022
Establishes the case for fusing separate modality-specific models rather than training one model on everything — the same architectural choice MediLens makes.
A Review on Multimodal Medical Image Fusion
arXiv, 2021
Surveys fusion strategies (early/late/hybrid) — relevant background for how MediLens's combined risk summary could evolve beyond simple score averaging.
Explainability (Grad-CAM)
Grad-CAM: Visual Explanations from Deep Networks via Gradient-based Localization
Selvaraju et al., ICCV 2017
The foundational technique behind the "what the model looked at" heatmap concept planned for MediLens's X-ray and CT results.
Explainable AI for medical imaging: a survey
npj Digital Medicine, 2021
Grounds why explainability (not just a confidence score) matters for clinical trust — the motivation behind showing contributing factors, not just a percentage.
ECG-specific
Cardiologist-level arrhythmia detection with deep neural networks
Hannun et al., Nature Medicine, 2019
Benchmark work on classifying arrhythmias (AFL, RBBB, NSR, etc.) from single-lead ECG — directly relevant to Nidhi's classification approach.
Heart Rate Variability as a Biomarker in Health and Disease
Frontiers in Physiology, 2021
Background on HRV metrics (RMSSD, SDNN, LF/HF ratio) shown as contributing factors in MediLens's ECG results.
CT-specific
Automated detection of COVID-19 cases using deep neural networks with X-ray/CT images
Scientific Reports, 2020
Direct precedent for transfer-learning a CNN (ResNet-family) for COVID vs. non-COVID classification, the approach used in MediLens's CT model.
Deep Learning-Based Detection for COVID-19 from Chest CT using Transfer Learning
IEEE Access, 2020
Compares transfer-learning backbones (including ResNet18) for CT-based COVID classification with small training sets, similar in scale to our ~2,481-image dataset.
